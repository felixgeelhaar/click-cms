# What your account can do

Not everyone who signs in sees the same admin. There are four kinds of account,
and yours decides two things: which items appear in the menu down the left, and
which buttons you get on the screens you can reach.

This page exists because the difference is easy to mistake for a fault. If a
colleague describes a button you cannot find, or a screenshot shows a menu
heading your own menu does not have, the usual reason is that your account is a
different kind — not that anything is broken.

The four kinds are **administrator**, **editor**, **author** and **viewer**.

## How to tell which one you have

The admin does not print your account type anywhere on screen, so the quickest
way to tell is to look at what you have been given.

**Look down the left-hand menu.**

![The Click CMS dashboard with the navigation menu down the left-hand side. The menu shows Dashboard on its own, then a heading reading CONTENT above Pages, Collections, Media, Builder and Submissions, then a heading reading MANAGE above Plugins, Marketplace, Users, Redirects, Menus, Themes, Settings and Updates, then a heading reading ACCOUNT above Profile.](images/dashboard.png)

- **If there is a heading reading MANAGE**, with Plugins, Users and Settings
  underneath it, you are an **administrator**. That group is built only for
  administrators, so its presence is a reliable test.
- **If your menu goes Dashboard, CONTENT, ACCOUNT and stops there**, you are an
  editor, an author or a viewer.

To separate those three, open any page from **Pages** and read the strip across
the top of the editor:

- **If you can publish and take a page down from that strip**, you are an
  **editor**.
- **If it says "Your account cannot publish. Ask an editor to put these changes
  live."**, you are an **author**.
- **If you cannot save at all** — you can open everything and read it, but the
  site refuses your changes — you are a **viewer**.

If you would rather not work it out, ask whoever set up your account. It is a
one-line answer and worth having written down.

## Administrator

Everything. An administrator is the only kind of account that can reach the
**MANAGE** group, which means it is the only one that can:

- change the site's navigation (**Menus**), its look (**Themes**) and its name
  (**Settings**),
- set up old-address-to-new-address forwarding (**Redirects**),
- create and remove accounts, and decide what kind each one is (**Users**),
- install extra features and update the software itself (**Plugins**,
  **Marketplace**, **Updates**).

The last two of those can change what code the site runs, which is why they sit
behind the account type with the most trust. If you are the only person looking
after a small site, this is probably you. If your site is looked after by an
agency or a developer, it is probably them.

## Editor

Full control over content, and nothing beyond content. An editor can:

- create pages and collection entries, and edit **anybody's**, not only their
  own,
- publish and take down,
- preview unpublished work and share the link,
- restore an earlier version,
- upload pictures.

An editor cannot manage accounts, install anything, or change the menus, the
theme or the settings. This is the account most staff who look after a site
day to day will hold.

**One limit that surprises people:** an editor can delete only their own pages
and entries. Somebody else's page can be edited freely, published, taken down
and restored — but not deleted. Take it down instead; that removes it from the
public site and keeps everything. See
[Publishing your changes](publishing.md#taking-a-page-down).

## Author

An author writes and looks after **their own work only**, and cannot put it in
front of the public.

An author can create pages and entries, edit and delete the ones they made,
restore an earlier version of them, upload pictures, and take a preview link to
show a draft to somebody.

An author **cannot publish and cannot take down**. This is deliberate, not a
fault: an author drafts, and somebody else decides it goes live. It is the shape
you want when one person writes and another approves.

So, plainly: **if you are an author, pressing Save has not published anything,
and no amount of pressing it will.** Save your work, leave a note in the
comments box, and tell whoever approves that it is ready. The preview link exists
for exactly that handover — see
[Publishing your changes](publishing.md#leaving-a-note).

An author also cannot open somebody else's page for editing. If you need a
change to a page you did not create, ask.

## Viewer

Read-only. A viewer can sign in, look at content and look at the media library,
and change nothing.

A viewer cannot preview either. That is worth a sentence, because it looks like
an oversight and is not: a preview link shows unpublished work to whoever holds
the link, including people with no account at all. Handing that out is a
decision, and the read-only account is not the one to make it.

Viewer is also what an account falls back to if its type is not recognised — so
if somebody's account has suddenly gone read-only, that is the thing to ask
about.

## Two things this means in practice

**A visible button is not a promise.** A few buttons are shown to everyone
regardless of account type — **Delete** in the Pages list is the one to know
about. If your account may not do it, the site refuses when you click, with a
message saying so. Nothing bad happens; the action does not go through.

**"Add it to the menu" is an administrator's job.** The site's navigation lives
on the **Menus** screen, which only an administrator can reach. If you are an
editor or an author and you have been asked to put a new page in the navigation,
you cannot do it yourself — make the page, publish it if you are allowed to, and
ask an administrator to add it. That is not a limitation you can work around, so
it is better known early than discovered late.
